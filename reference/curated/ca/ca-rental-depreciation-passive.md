## Rules

### Depreciation Differences
- Use FTB 3885A only if California depreciation or amortization differs from federal. File one FTB 3885A for each business or activity with a difference. [2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Purpose; Specific Line Instructions]
- California does not conform to the federal additional first-year (bonus) depreciation under IRC Section 168(k), including the OBBBA 100% allowance. [FTB Pub. 1001 (2025), Additional depreciation (IRC Section 168(k)); 2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Federal/State Differences]
- The maximum IRC Section 179 expense deduction for California is $25,000. [2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Part II]
- If the activity is passive, FTB 3885A is a worksheet whose result carries to FTB 3801. [2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Part I Line 1]
- The FTB 3885A list of federal and state differences is not all-inclusive (examples listed include different recovery periods for property placed in service before 1997 and the 30-year alternative depreciation system period). For a difference not listed in Pub. 1001 or the FTB 3885A instructions: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov". [FTB Pub. 1001 (2025); 2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Federal/State Differences]
- Conservative default: take California depreciation as if no bonus applied, so California depreciation is less than federal. The adjustment is total California depreciation compared with total federal depreciation. Where California depreciation is less than federal, the difference goes to Schedule CA (540NR) column C; for a passive rental it goes through FTB 3885A to FTB 3801. [2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Part III Lines 8a and 8b; FTB Pub. 1001 (2025), Additional depreciation (IRC Section 168(k))]

### Passive Activity Rules
- For California, all rental activities are passive. The federal real-property-business exception (IRC Section 469(c)(7)) does not apply. [2025 FTB 3801 Instructions, Material Participation in Real Property Business; Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Line 5]
- Use FTB 3801 to figure the line 5 adjustment when passive activities produce a loss; use FTB 3885A when only nonpassive activities, or passive activities with gains, are involved. [Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Line 5]
- A $25,000 special allowance exists for losses from active participation in rental real estate activities, subject to the income phase-out on FTB 3801; for married/RDP filing separately who lived apart all year the figure is $75,000 on line 5 instead of $150,000, and those who lived together at any time in the year are not eligible. Whether this filer qualifies: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov" beyond the FTB 3801 worksheet. [2025 FTB 3801 Instructions, Line 5; FTB Pub. 1100 (2025), Passive Activities]

### Part-Year Residents and Rental Property
- A part-year resident computes suspended passive losses as if a California resident for all prior years and as if a nonresident for all prior years, then prorates both by the periods of California residency and nonresidency. [2025 FTB 3801 Instructions, Part-Year Resident; FTB Pub. 1100 (2025), Passive Activities, Part-Year Resident]
- Rental profit or loss: resident amounts are those that accrued while a California resident; nonresident amounts are limited to property located in California. [Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Line 5, part-year resident amounts]
- The actual-date-of-realization and daily pro-rata allocation rule is stated for partnership, S corporation and certain trust income, not for direct rental income. For a direct rental, allocating rents by the period earned and expenses by the period incurred is the conservative reading; a daily pro-rata split of a direct rental: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov". [Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Line 5, part-year resident amounts]
- Rental income appears in column D for the whole year (resident-for-all-year presentation); only the resident-period part appears in column E for property located outside California. [2025 Form 540NR Booklet, General Information; Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Column E]
- Whether expenses paid before a converted property was available for rent are deductible: no provided FTB material addresses this. "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov".

### Sale of Rental Property
- For a nonresident, California taxes real property based on where the property is located; California taxes residents on all income regardless of source. A part-year resident's gain on property outside California is therefore taxable if realized while a California resident and not taxable if realized as a nonresident. [FTB Pub. 1100 (2025), Installment Sales, Examples 4-7]
