## Rules

### Depreciation Differences
- Use FTB 3885A only if California depreciation or amortization differs from federal. File one FTB 3885A for each business or activity with a difference. [2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Purpose; Specific Line Instructions]
- California does not conform to the federal additional first-year (bonus) depreciation under IRC Section 168(k), including the OBBBA 100% allowance. [FTB Pub. 1001 (2025), Additional depreciation (IRC Section 168(k)); 2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Federal/State Differences]
- The maximum IRC Section 179 expense deduction for California is $25,000. [2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Part II]
- If the activity is passive, FTB 3885A is a worksheet whose result carries to FTB 3801. [2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Part I Line 1]
- Differences in recovery period or method for ordinary (non-bonus) depreciation exist only where Pub. 1001 or the FTB 3885A instructions list them. Any difference not listed there: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov". [FTB Pub. 1001 (2025); 2025 FTB 3885A Instructions, Federal/State Differences]
- Conservative default: add back federal bonus depreciation in Schedule CA (540NR) column C and take California depreciation as if no bonus applied. [FTB Pub. 1001 (2025), Additional depreciation (IRC Section 168(k))]

### Passive Activity Rules
- For California, all rental activities are passive. The federal real-property-business exception (IRC Section 469(c)(7)) does not apply. [2025 FTB 3801 Instructions, Material Participation in Real Property Business; Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Line 5]
- Use FTB 3801 to figure the line 5 adjustment when passive activities produce a loss; use FTB 3885A when only nonpassive activities, or passive activities with gains, are involved. [Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Line 5]
- Passive loss limits allow up to $25,000 of rental losses after netting passive income and losses (Pub. 1100 example). [FTB Pub. 1100 (2025), Passive Activities, Always a Nonresident example]

### Part-Year Residents and Rental Property
- A part-year resident computes suspended passive losses as if a California resident for all prior years and as if a nonresident for all prior years, then prorates both by the periods of California residency and nonresidency. [2025 FTB 3801 Instructions, Part-Year Resident; FTB Pub. 1100 (2025), Passive Activities, Part-Year Resident]
- Rental profit or loss: resident amounts are those that accrued while a California resident; nonresident amounts are limited to property located in California. Allocate by actual realization date, or by daily pro-rata if no better information exists. [Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Line 5, part-year resident amounts]
- Rental income appears in column D for the whole year (resident-for-all-year presentation); only the resident-period part appears in column E for property located outside California. [2025 Form 540NR Booklet, General Information; Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Column E]
- Whether expenses paid before a converted property was available for rent are deductible is a federal characterization question; California follows the federal result unless an FTB source says otherwise: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov".

### Sale of Rental Property
- California taxes real property based on where the property is located. [FTB Pub. 1100 (2025), Installment Sales]
