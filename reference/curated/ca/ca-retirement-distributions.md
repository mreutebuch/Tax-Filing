## Rules

### Nonresident Period
- California does not tax retirement income received by a nonresident after December 31, 1995. This includes military pensions, IRA distributions, Roth IRA conversions, Roth IRA distributions, SEP plans and self-employed retirement plans (Keoghs). Private deferred compensation counts as retirement income only if it meets the conditions in Pub. 1005; nonqualified deferred compensation has its own Schedule CA (540NR) line. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Nonresidents of California Receiving a California Pension; Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Lines 4a-4b and 5a-5b]
- A nonresident includes no IRA distributions and no qualified retirement distributions in Schedule CA (540NR) column E. [Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Lines 4a-4b and 5a-5b, California nonresident amounts]

### Part-Year Residents
- A part-year resident is taxed on all income received while a resident and only on California-source income while a nonresident. A pension earned for services outside California has a non-California source, so only the part received while a resident is taxable. Pub. 1005 example: a $2,000 monthly pension, resident for 8 months, gives a taxable portion of $16,000 ($2,000 x 8 months). [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Example 4]
- For IRA distributions received while a resident, the rules for California residents apply, except that a former nonresident's IRA basis follows the 2002 law-change rule below. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), IRA Distribution, Nonresidents of California, Change of Residency]
- Record the date of each 1099-R distribution; an annual 1099-R total cannot be split by residency period without the distribution dates. Retirement income is included in California source income only if you were a California resident on the date of the distribution. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Roth IRA Conversions, part-year resident; Example 4; Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Part-Year Resident Worksheet]

### IRA Basis After Moving to California
- A former nonresident who becomes a California resident is treated as a resident for all prior years for deferred income, including IRAs. California IRA basis is allowed only for contributions that would not have been deductible under California law had the filer been a California resident. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), 2002 Law Changes IRA Basis of Former Nonresidents]
- Adjustment when California basis differs from federal basis: if the federal taxable amount is more than the California amount, enter the difference in column B on Schedule CA (540NR) line 4b; if less, enter the difference in column C. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), IRA Distribution, Nondeductible Contributions Made After 1986]
- Pub. 1005 Worksheet I figures pre-1987 California basis and the line 4b adjustment; post-1986 nondeductible contributions follow federal Form 8606 instructions using California amounts. Both need the filer's contribution and deduction history; where that history is missing: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov". [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Worksheet I; IRA Distribution, Nondeductible Contributions Made After 1986]

### Roth IRA
- California conforms to federal law for Roth IRAs. No California adjustment is needed unless a traditional IRA was converted and its California basis differs from its federal basis (or a 1998 conversion was spread over 4 years). [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Roth IRA, In General and Roth IRA Distributions]
- Roth IRA conversions use the Roth IRA Worksheet in Pub. 1005 when California basis differs. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Roth IRA Conversions]

### Additional Tax on Early Distributions (FTB 3805P)
- Generally, a distribution before age 59½ from a qualified plan, IRA, annuity contract or modified endowment contract is an early distribution. The part included in income is subject to an additional 2½% tax (the California rate), figured on FTB 3805P. A SIMPLE retirement plan distribution received in the first two years of participation is subject to an additional 6% tax. [2025 FTB 3805P Instructions, Definitions, Early Distributions]
- California does not conform to all of the federal exceptions to the early-distribution tax. File FTB 3805P when an early taxable distribution from a qualified retirement plan has a Box 7 code other than 2, 3 or 4; when you owe tax on an early distribution but Box 7 incorrectly shows an exception code; or when an exception applies but code 2, 3 or 4 is not shown or is incorrect (file even if no tax is owed). You need not file when an exception applies and code 2, 3 or 4 is correctly shown. [2025 FTB 3805P Instructions, Who Must File; General Information]
- FTB 3805P Line 2 lists the California exceptions by exception number (01 through 25, and 99 when more than one applies). Use a distribution only if it fits that list. The provided FTB materials do not map 1099-R Box 7 codes to those exception numbers, so the mapping is unverified: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov". [2025 FTB 3805P Instructions, Line 2]
- For Form 540NR filers the amount entered on FTB 3805P line 1 is the taxable amount reported on Schedule CA (540NR) line 4b or 5b, so an early distribution received during a nonresident period (not in column E) does not enter the additional tax. Annuity contract and modified endowment contract distributions are not covered by Pub. 1005's retirement-income definition: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov". [2025 FTB 3805P Instructions, Line 1; FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Nonresidents of California Receiving a California Pension]
