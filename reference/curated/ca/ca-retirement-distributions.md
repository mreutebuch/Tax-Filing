## Rules

### Nonresident Period
- California does not tax retirement income received by a nonresident after December 31, 1995. This includes IRA distributions, Roth IRA conversions, Roth IRA distributions and pensions. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Nonresidents of California Receiving a California Pension]
- A nonresident includes no IRA or pension income in Schedule CA (540NR) column E. [Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Lines 4a-4b and 5a-5b, California nonresident amounts]

### Part-Year Residents
- A part-year resident is taxed on all income received while a resident and only on California-source income while a nonresident. A pension earned for services outside California has a non-California source, so only the part received while a resident is taxable. Pub. 1005 example: a $2,000 monthly pension, resident for 8 months, gives a taxable portion of $16,000 ($2,000 x 8 months). [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Example 4]
- For IRA distributions received while a resident, the rules for California residents apply. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), IRA Distribution, Nonresidents of California, Change of Residency]
- Record the date of each 1099-R distribution; an annual 1099-R total cannot be split by residency period without the distribution dates. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Example 4; Schedule CA (540NR) Instructions, Part-Year Resident Worksheet]

### IRA Basis After Moving to California
- A former nonresident who becomes a California resident is treated as a resident for all prior years for deferred income, including IRAs. California IRA basis is allowed only for contributions that would not have been deductible under California law had the filer been a California resident. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), 2002 Law Changes IRA Basis of Former Nonresidents]
- Adjustment when California basis differs from federal basis: if the federal taxable amount is more than the California amount, enter the difference in column B on Schedule CA (540NR) line 4b; if less, enter the difference in column C. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), IRA Distribution, Nondeductible Contributions Made After 1986]
- Pub. 1005 Worksheet I figures California basis. It needs the filer's contribution and deduction history; where that history is missing: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov". [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Worksheet I]

### Roth IRA
- California conforms to federal law for Roth IRAs. No California adjustment is needed unless a traditional IRA was converted and its California basis differs from its federal basis (or a 1998 conversion was spread over 4 years). [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Roth IRA, In General and Roth IRA Distributions]
- Roth IRA conversions use the Roth IRA Worksheet in Pub. 1005 when California basis differs. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Roth IRA Conversions]

### Additional Tax on Early Distributions (FTB 3805P)
- Generally, a distribution before age 59½ from a qualified plan or IRA is an early distribution. The part included in income is subject to an additional 2½% tax (the California rate), figured on FTB 3805P. [2025 FTB 3805P Instructions, Definitions, Early Distributions]
- California conforms to the federal exceptions only in part. Distribution codes 2, 3 or 4 in 1099-R Box 7 show an exception. File FTB 3805P when an early taxable distribution has a Box 7 code other than 2, 3 or 4, or when an exception applies but code 2, 3 or 4 is missing or wrong. [2025 FTB 3805P Instructions, General Information B, Who Must File]
- Whether a specific distribution meets a California exception depends on its facts and code: "I cannot verify this from provided FTB materials — check ftb.ca.gov" unless the instructions list the exception.
- Early distributions received during a nonresident period are not California income, so the additional tax does not apply to them. [FTB Pub. 1005 (2025), Nonresidents of California Receiving a California Pension]
